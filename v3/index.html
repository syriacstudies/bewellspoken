You are not the only one

Every family arrives with a version of one of these

My three year old uses maybe ten words. My mother says wait. My gut says no. Help me make sense of it.

The 9pm worry

Her preschool teacher said the word stuttering and I have been replaying every video of her ever since.

The teacher comment

He understands every word we say. He just does not talk yet. We are worried, and also a little embarrassed to be worried.

The quiet one

We have tried everything except actually asking someone who knows.

The reason this site exists

If one of these sounds like a thought you have had, you are in the right place. None of them are silly, and none of them are automatically a problem. They are reasons to look a little closer, calmly.

“Wait and see” is not the only option.
Panic is not the only alternative.

There is a middle path: learn what is typical, organize what you are noticing, and have one good conversation with a professional. This site exists to walk you down that path.

How families use this place

A calm path from worry to clarity

Get your bearings

Look up your child's age on the milestones page. Most parents find at least one thing that reassures them and one thing worth watching.

Organize what you see

Take the check-in. It turns a fog of 2am worries into a clear, printable summary of what you are noticing and how long you have noticed it.

Have one good conversation

Bring the summary to your pediatrician or a speech-language pathologist. A short conversation with the right person beats months of wondering.

Your pediatrician stays in the loop here, always. For some concerns, like hearing or feeding or anything medical, the pediatrician is the right first call. Nothing on this site replaces that relationship.

Who is behind this

Reviewed by a real clinician, not scraped from the internet

Well Spoken is a small family project, run with a licensed, ASHA-certified speech-language pathologist in Bergen County, New Jersey. She has spent her career doing evaluations and therapy with children, in schools and in clinics, and answering exactly these questions for friends at exactly those birthday parties.

Every milestone, every check-in question, and every article on this site passes through her review before it is published. When something is debated among clinicians, we say so. When the honest answer is "talk to a professional," we say that too.

Pediatricians, teachers, and directors
