The Feature

When a quiet three year old is not really a quiet three year old.

By Jill Benyamine, MA, CCC-SLP

You have been doing the counting at birthday parties. How many words she uses, roughly, compared to the girl across the kitchen. You have been watching her brother repeat himself three times and still not be understood at the door. You have mentioned it, once, to the pediatrician, and the answer was a version of "let us see in six months." Six months is a long time when you are the one watching.

Here is what I can tell you as the person who spends her days with children who are finding their words. Waiting is sometimes the right call, and sometimes it is the most expensive thing a family can do. The difference is almost never obvious from across a room. It takes a conversation and a set of notes, the kind you have been keeping already.

The Column
A note from the editor

You were right to look into this. Now let me help.

I am Jill. I am a speech-language pathologist, I trained in New York, and for ten years I worked in schools and clinics in Bergen County watching the same thing happen. A parent notices something at two. The system hears about it at four. A lot of small conversations would have gone a long way, and they never happened, because nobody knew who to ask.

Well Spoken is that first conversation. You send me a short note. I read it, not a form algorithm, and I write back. If we should keep going, I personally match you to an evaluator in my network. If we should not, I tell you that too, and often that is the whole answer.

Jill
MA, CCC-SLP · Licensed in New Jersey and New York
